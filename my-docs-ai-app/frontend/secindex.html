Exploring the Social Science Disciplines

Economics and Political Science in Higher Education Policy

Overview of the Discipline: Economics

  • Economics studies how people, firms, and governments allocate limited resources and weigh the costs and benefits of decisions such as investing in higher education.
Image placeholder, economics overview

Two Key Theories in Economics

  • Human capital theory: Eide and Showalter (2010) explain human capital theory as treating education like a business investment, where a person takes on direct and indirect costs now in exchange for higher earnings later. Reber and Turner (2026) apply this to income driven repayment, showing how payments are spread across a career instead of paid up front.
  • Moral hazard and risk sharing theory: McGuire and van Kleef (2018) describe risk sharing as an arrangement where a regulator absorbs part of the financial risk in a system, which protects participants but can also weaken their incentive to control costs. Dandridge (2026) and Reber and Turner (2026) apply this same tension to tuition pricing and student borrowing.
Image placeholder, human capital and risk sharing theory

Purpose and Importance of Economics, with Example

  • Economics helps measure whether money spent on college produces a real return for students and taxpayers. A clear example is the shift from the SAVE plan to the new Repayment Assistance Plan, which calculates payments differently for low income versus high income graduates.

Overview of the Discipline: Political Science

  • Political science studies how governments, agencies, laws, and interest groups make decisions and distribute resources like access to higher education across society.
Image placeholder, political science overview

Two Key Theories in Political Science

  • Institutionalism, the regulatory state approach: Hoefer (2022) states "structures endure while individuals come and go" (para. 9). This idea is central to institutionalism and explains why policy tends to stay in place even as administrations change. Dandridge (2026) applies this to how regulators in England and the United States enforce academic quality through binding rules.
  • Agenda setting theory, related to policy feedback theory: Mohn (2022) states "the media does not necessarily tell people what they should think" (para. 5). This same logic extends to agenda setting in policy. Reber and Turner (2026) trace how litigation and new legislation keep reshaping student loan repayment depending on who controls Congress and the courts.
Image placeholder, institutionalism and agenda setting theory

Purpose and Importance of Political Science, with Example

  • Political science explains how rules get written and how shifts in political power change who has access to affordable college. A strong example is how state attorneys general challenged an executive loan forgiveness plan in court, forcing Congress to pass new legislation.
Image placeholder, attorneys general and Congress example

Key Takeaway

  • Dandridge (2026) states "quality is a fundamental principle of higher education" (para. 1). That line ties both disciplines together, since economics asks whether quality produces a measurable return and political science asks who gets to define and enforce it.
